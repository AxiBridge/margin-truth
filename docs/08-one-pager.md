# Margin Truth

**Quote-time pricing for service businesses that use up product on every job.**

---

## The problem

A detailer quotes $275 for a full detail because that's what he charged last time and it
felt right. His floor on that job — his own pay, his truck, his insurance, his product —
is $378. He lost $103 and felt good about it.

QuickBooks tells him this in March, in aggregate, after the year is gone. Nothing tells him
at the moment he says the number out loud.

## Who

Micro service businesses where a meaningful share of job cost gets consumed and nobody
tracks it: detailing, pressure and soft washing, lawn and turf, pest control, window
cleaning, pool service.

Not consultants, not bookkeepers, not trades billing hours or passing materials at cost.

## What it does

Pick a service, get a price. The price is built from real numbers: the operator's pay,
overhead allocated per billable hour, and product consumed at their dilution and their
supplier cost.

It shows the floor — the price below which the job costs money to perform. You can price
above it. You cannot price below it without saying why.

## Why it works

Every operator believes they're profitable and can't prove it. The first time they
retro-price a job they were proud of and watch it come back underwater, the tool is
installed for good.

Then it closes the loop: quoted margin against realised margin, every month, so the gap
from drive time, rework, over-application and phone discounts becomes visible for the
first time.

## The moat

Coverage rates. Knowing that a full detail burns three cutting pads and twelve ounces of
shampoo comes from having done the work. It can't be scraped, and a wrong rate produces a
wrong price, so it also caps how fast the product can expand. No vertical ships without
real rate data.

## Distribution

The founder's detailing network first. Detailers and pressure washers share chemicals,
dilution logic, and frequently the same operator, so the first two verticals come from
the same relationships.

## Where it goes

The floor is the wedge. From there: send the quote, take the payment, book the job — each
one feeding the operator's realised-margin record, which after a year is the thing they
cannot take anywhere else. The endgame is the field-service platform for trades that use
up product, priced alongside Urable and OrbisX, with a number none of them can show.

## Status

Demo built, September 2026. Founder's own pack shows six of ten services priced below
floor. Validating with five operators before launch build.
